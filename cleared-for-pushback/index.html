LT 7100 · Instructional Design

Cleared for Pushback: A Formative Evaluation Story

A lesson for LT 7100, Instructional Design, in the Georgia State University M.S. Instructional Design & Technology program. It opens with what formative evaluation is and why it happens before rollout, then follows a four-person design team at a fictional Atlanta airline as they evaluate Safe Ramp, a new onboarding course for ramp agents, through one-to-one, small group and field trial testing.

9:22Graphic-novel motion comic

What it covers

Each step opens with a setup screen and a short definition, then shows the Peachline team doing it.

1 · Background & Context

The problem the course is meant to solve, who the learners are, and the launch decision the results will inform.

2 · Evaluation Questions

Four questions about mastery, usability, time and trust, each with a criterion set before any data is collected.

3 · Data Collection Methods

Three rounds of testing, with example protocols for the one-to-one interview and observation, the small group session, and the field trial.

4 · Data Analysis

Summarizing scores and times, coding comments into themes, and checking whether the sources agree.

5 · Results

What each round found, and how a ten-point gap between two stations turned out to be about access, not instruction.

6 · Recommendations

Must-fix and nice-to-have changes, each tied to evidence, and the client's decision to launch with a summative evaluation to follow.

How it was made

Design

  • Script written with the instructor around the six-step formative evaluation process and the course textbook
  • Painted graphic-novel style, between comic book and photorealistic, with approved character sheets for the five main characters

Media

  • 28 keyframes generated with GPT Image 2.5 on Higgsfield, each built from the character sheets so the team stays consistent
  • AI narration (Higgsfield Seed Audio), with an original ambient music bed composed in code; loudness set to −16 LUFS

Build

  • Setup screens, protocol documents and data captions are drawn in code, so every word and number is exact
  • Slow camera moves and dissolves assembled with FFmpeg behind the branded LT 7100 splash
Images and narration were generated with AI and reviewed by the instructor. Peachline Airways, Peachline Learning Academy and everyone in the video are fictional, and the evaluation data is invented for teaching. The “cook tastes the soup” line is Robert Stake’s well-known description of formative and summative evaluation.
