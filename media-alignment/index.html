LT 7100 · Instructional Design

How to Recognize Instructional Media Alignment with Learning Goals

The companion lesson for LT 7100, Instructional Design, in the Georgia State University M.S. Instructional Design & Technology program. Function first, form follows: this video shows how to judge media against the objective rather than against taste.

3:47Narrated lessonDownload MP4

What it covers

1 · What alignment means

Constructive alignment applied to media: it has to serve both the objective (what the learner must be able to do) and the performance conditions (where, with what, and under what pressure).

2 · Three signs of misalignment

How to spot media that only looks aligned, including motion or detail the task doesn't need and designs that split the learner's attention.

Worked examples

A phishing course that needs a practice simulation rather than a talking avatar, and a lockout-tagout procedure where five annotated photos beat video and double as a job aid.

How it was made

Design

  • Script written and locked by the instructor, grounded in Mayer's multimedia learning research
  • A deliberately calm, Vox-style paper-collage look: no fly-ins, elements fade up in place, motion only where motion is the content

Media

  • Still images from GPT Image 2.5 against a single style frame; two short B-roll clips from Grok Video 1.5
  • Narration in a Higgsfield preset voice, with pauses tightened and loudness set to −16 LUFS

Build

  • A GSAP scene engine in HTML, with every label timed to the spoken word by audio alignment
  • Rendered frame by frame in headless Chromium and assembled with FFmpeg behind the branded LT 7100 splash
